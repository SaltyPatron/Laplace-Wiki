# Hops

The lexicons are separate witnesses, and a hop is the curated edge that lets consensus on a claim in one of them pull on a claim in another.

A hop is not a third lexicon. It states that a concept, frame, roleset, sense, or entity named in one resource corresponds to one named in another, joining shared **highway IDs**—content-addressed identifiers (`i46360`, `abandon.01`, `leave-51.2`, `Abandonment`, `va:0001f`, `Q23622`, `eng`) on which every citing witness lands ([Corpora](README.md#page-shape), [30. Conflicts](../Sequence/Conflicts.md) S1, I5). Where a mapping resource cites an edition-local internal pointer (a WordNet 1.6 or 3.0 byte offset, a sense key `abandon%2:40:00::`, a BabelNet ID, or a decomposed FrameBase/WordNet RDF URI), the recipe decomposes that notation, resolves it through `highway.bin` to the target highway node or `[lemma, language, ILI]` lexicalization strand, and records the pointer itself nowhere.

Each resource declares its highway types and crosswalks via `types`, `keyed`, `alias`, and `maps` directives read by `laplace highway` into `highway.bin` ([Types](../Reference/Types.md#perf-caches), [Claims](../Semantics/Claims.md#the-linguistic-super-highway)), while `laplace ingest` records each mapping row as one attested interchange strand under that witness's trust (and precision/confidence score where published) so Highway A* ([20. Forward](../Sequence/Forward.md), [Pull](../Semantics/Pull.md#hop-and-fanout)) can traverse across vocabularies and languages in microseconds.
