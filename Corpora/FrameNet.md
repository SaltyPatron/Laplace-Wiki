# FrameNet

FrameNet attests what its XML says of each frame, frame element, lexical unit, semantic type, corpus, document, sentence, annotation set, lemma, lexeme, and word form, each named by its kind and its name together, and the release's README, book, and schemas attest nothing.

One source reads FrameNet: Release 1.7 of the Berkeley FrameNet data, in the form NLTK redistributes, "frames with their frame elements and lexical units, the relations between frames, the semantic types, the lemma and lexeme tables, and the annotated sentences of the lexical units and of the full texts". Every file of it is XML, read as what it says: the recipe names which elements are things and what names each, and everything else follows from how the file wrote it. The project's site is <https://framenet.icsi.berkeley.edu/>.

## The things

FrameNet numbers each kind of thing from one. The recipe says why that matters: "the number 4489 is a frame element, a lexical unit, a lemma, a lexeme and a word form, five different things. So a thing is named by its kind and its name together, as FrameNet's own attributes do when they refer to one (feID, luID, lemmaID, frameName): [FE, 4489], [frame, Abandonment]." A thing is the path of its kind and its name; the kind is the element's name unless the recipe says the release writes it under another. Every name and value is as the files write them.

A thing's own attributes and text are one record, witnessed once, and every thing inside it is a record of its own. An element that is no thing is one record: the path of its name, its claims, and the records of the elements inside it. A claim that stands alone is its own record. Nothing is renamed, reordered, or filled in.
