# Wordnets

The wordnets attest sense: what each says of a word, of a synset, which is its ILI and as built the words it lists, and of a sense that is a word in a synset, every value as the wordnet writes it; CILI is the highway's interlingual index, and every synset's `ili` is CILI's identifier, content, attesting that the synset is the concept CILI identifies by it; CILI's own file attests each concept's definition; the Princeton database files that the WN-LMF editions already carry are not read, except `lexnames`, the lexicographer files; and the manual pages attest nothing.

Four sources read the wordnets, in the order [`recipes/order`](https://github.com/SaltyPatron/Laplace-Engine/blob/main/recipes/order) gives: the Collaborative Interlingual Index, Open English WordNet, Open Multilingual Wordnet, then Princeton WordNet 3.0. A wordnet's ids for its entries, senses and synsets (`oewn-dog-n`, `oewn-02084071-n`, `example-en-10161911-n`, a synset offset) are its internal pointers: how the file points at its own records. They are decomposed by the wordnet's notation only for the facts they carry (`oewn-02084071-n` a prefix, an offset, and the part of speech `n`), resolve to the things they name, and are recorded nowhere ([Corpora](README.md#page-shape)). A WordNet 3.0 offset is the byte position of the synset's line in that release's `data.<pos>` file, unique only with its part of speech and only within its release. The `ili` is the highway node, CILI's identifier, content that every wordnet citing it lands on. A sense key (`abandon%2:40:00::`, a lemma, part of speech 2, lexicographer file 40, lex_id 00) is WordNet's internal pointer to a lexicalization: decomposed by WordNet's grammar for the facts it carries, resolved through the highway perf-cache to the lexicalization strand `[lemma, language, ILI]`, and recorded nowhere. Open Multilingual Wordnet is one source trunk and one witness: each lexicon, such as `omw-fr.xml`, is a file under that trunk with its path, and never a witness of its own. The Engine is built otherwise: it names a witness per lexicon by the label the lexicon writes (`witness {first label}`), and the target is the one trunk.

## WN-LMF

Open English WordNet and every wordnet of Open Multilingual Wordnet are one file each in WN-LMF, the Global WordNet Association's XML for wordnets, [WN-LMF-1.3.dtd](http://globalwordnet.github.io/schemas/WN-LMF-1.3.dtd). Both read like the shared [`wn-lmf.recipe`](https://github.com/SaltyPatron/Laplace-Engine/blob/main/recipes/wn-lmf.recipe), which says what each element is:

What one element says it says together, as one record: a thing's place in the thing it is inside, its own attributes, its own text; an element that is not a thing speaks of the thing it is inside. Every entry stands on lines of its own (`records`), so a long file is read in parts on every core.

Measured on Open English WordNet 2024 ([Research](../Research/README.md)): 2.70 million compositions and 1.77 million attestations, against 4.49 million when its ids were content.

## CILI

The Collaborative Interlingual Index: "language-independent concept identifiers, their definitions, and their maps to the wordnets. The definitions are Princeton WordNet's." Each concept is a type of the highway's `ili` list (116,698 of them). The concept is its identifier, `i46360`, a highway node, content: that it is an ILI is what `ili.ttl` attests, and its definition is said of it. As built, its content is the definition `ili.ttl` gives it, so that the 961 concepts that share a definition with another are one type each by content; the target is the identifier. The WordNet 3.0 offset of each concept comes from `ili.ttl` itself (`keyed ili … dc:source`, `pwn30:…`); `ili-map-pwn30.tab` is matched by no recipe and not read; and WordNet's `index.sense` the sense keys, so a wordnet's `ili`, VerbNet's `wn`, VerbAtlas's `wn:` and `bn:` spellings, PredicateMatrix's offsets all resolve to the same slot ([Hops](Hops.md)). As built the sense keys resolve to that slot too; in the target a sense key resolves to the lexicalization strand it points at. `ili.recipe` reads `ili.ttl`: a concept is its `skos:definition` (`thing triple … skos:definition …`), what each statement's `a` says is related of it, and a definition's language tag is paired with it. The sense mappings, the other maps and `changes-in-wn31.csv` match no recipe and are not read.

## The Princeton database

WordNet 3.0's `dict` files are the edition `omw-en.xml` carries in WN-LMF, which is read above. The database tables that say the same (`data.*`, `index.*`, `index.sense`, `sentidx.vrb`) are not read as tables; `lexnames` is, by `lexnames.recipe`: each lexicographer file is its name, its number a pointer (the number the sense keys and the data files write), resolved to the file and recorded nowhere, its syntactic category attested of it, and it is a type of the highway's `lexfile` list; `index.sense` gives the highway only its sense keys (`index-sense.recipe`, `alias` lines).

| File | Laplace reads it as | Claim recorded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `cntlist`, `cntlist.rev` | the sense the sense key points at: the key is WordNet's internal pointer to a lexicalization, resolved through the highway perf-cache to the lexicalization strand and recorded nowhere (as built, resolved to the ILI concept, `type sense_key ili`); its tag count and its sense number are observations of that sense, numbers the file states, content and not games | `[sense, tag_cnt, 10742]`, `[sense, sense_number, 1]`, observations |
| `noun.exc`, `verb.exc`, `adj.exc`, `adv.exc` | the inflected form, and each base form after it, under the file's category | `[inflected form, noun, base form]` |
| the manual pages, `README`, `LICENSE`, the HTML | text, observed | nothing |
